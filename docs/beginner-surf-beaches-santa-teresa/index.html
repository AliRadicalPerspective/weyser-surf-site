Best beginner surf beaches near Santa Teresa.

For first timers, the best beaches are Playa Hermosa and Playa Carmen: wide, sandy beach breaks with gentle whitewater and plenty of room to practice. Playa Santa Teresa, the main beach in town, works for every level depending on the tide and time of day, and Mar Azul, a reef break south of Malpaís, is for intermediate surfers. Weyser picks the beach each morning based on the tide and swell, so you always learn wherever the waves are best that day.

Best for first timers

Playa Hermosa

A long, wide, sandy beach just north of Santa Teresa. The waves are softer here and the lineup is quieter, so there’s room to practice. Best from mid to high tide. Stay clear of the channel by the north point, where there’s a rip current and rocks.

Best all-rounder

Playa Carmen

Right at the main crossroads between Santa Teresa and Malpaís. A sandy beach break with peaks along the whole beach and forgiving whitewater. It’s the busiest spot, so Weyser finds a quieter peak, and a few rocks show at low tide.

All levels, by tide and time

Playa Santa Teresa

The main beach in town and the most powerful of the four. On small days and at the right tide, the whitewater close to shore works for beginners. With more swell, especially from mid to low tide, it gets fast and hollow: a wave for intermediate and expert surfers. Weyser picks the tide and time of day to match your level.

PHOTO NEEDEDMar Azul: the beach and the big rock at high tide.
For intermediate surfers

Mar Azul

South of Malpaís, by the big rock. A reef break over rock that only works around high tide with a bigger swell. It’s an intermediate beach: not for a first lesson, but a great next step once you’re riding green waves. At low tide, it’s known for its tide pools.

Which beach is best for beginners in Santa Teresa?

Playa Hermosa and Playa Carmen. Both are sandy beach breaks with gentle whitewater. On small days, the main beach at Playa Santa Teresa works too, at the right tide. Weyser picks the beach each morning based on the tide and swell.

Why does the tide matter?

The same beach can be perfect at one tide and rough at another. For beginners, Playa Hermosa and Playa Carmen are usually easiest from mid to high tide. Weyser checks the forecast every day and sends you the time and beach the evening before.

Can beginners surf at Mar Azul?

Not for a first lesson. Mar Azul is an intermediate beach: it breaks over rock and reef and needs a high tide and bigger swell. Weyser takes students there once they’re comfortable on green waves.

Is it crowded?

Playa Carmen gets busy, especially in high season. Playa Hermosa is usually quieter. With three people max, Weyser can take you to a quieter peak, and he teaches you how to share the waves.

When is the best time of year for beginners?

The dry season, roughly December to April, usually brings smaller, cleaner waves that are easiest to learn on. In the green season the swell is bigger, so Weyser sticks to the gentlest beaches and tides.
