Your first surf lesson in Santa Teresa: what to expect.

Your first lesson with Weyser, who has taught 800+ lessons here, is about 90 minutes in the water plus a beach briefing, in a group of three people max. You start in the whitewater on a big soft board, and most people stand up in their first session. The board, rashguard and wax are included, and pickup in town is $5: you just bring swimwear, sunscreen and water.

How the lesson goes, step by step

  1. The day before

    Tell him your level on WhatsApp

    Weyser checks the tide and swell, picks the beach that suits beginners that day, and sends you the time the evening before.

  2. Pickup

    He picks you up in town

    Anywhere in Santa Teresa for $5 per person. Or meet him at the beach if you’re staying close by.

  3. On the sand, about 15 minutes

    Beach briefing

    How the waves work, where to stand, how to fall safely, and a few rules for sharing the waves. Then you practice the pop-up on the sand until it feels natural.

  4. In the water, about 90 minutes

    Catching waves in the whitewater

    You start in the broken waves close to shore, where you can stand. He pushes you into waves at the right moment and fixes your pop-up after every try. With three people max, he sees every single wave you catch.

  5. The best part

    Your first ride

    Most people stand up in their first session. It's the moment everyone remembers.

  6. After

    A fresh coconut and what's next

    A fresh coconut is included. If you added photos of your session (for an additional fee), he sends them on WhatsApp, and he tells you what to work on next.

What to bring

Weyser has the rest.

  • Swimwear you can move in
  • Reef-safe sunscreen
  • Water to drink
  • A towel
  • A hair tie if you have long hair
  • Leave valuables at home

Will I stand up in my first lesson?

Most people do. You start in the whitewater, where the waves are small and slow, and Weyser times each push for you. You'll feel the difference after just one lesson.

Is it hard?

Paddling is the tiring part, and your arms will know about it. In the whitewater you mostly stand and walk, so you don’t need to be fit, just comfortable in the water.

Do I need to be a strong swimmer?

Not at all, you just need to be comfortable in the water. Beginners stay where they can stand, on a big, floaty soft board.

What if it rains or the waves are bad?

A little rain is no problem, you're getting wet anyway! Lightning is different: if there's lightning, Weyser always reschedules, no questions asked. Same if the waves aren't safe or fun. He'll move you to the next good day, free.

What board will I use?

A big soft-top foam board. It's stable, it floats well, and it won't hurt if it bumps you.

How do I pay?

PayPal or cash, on the day. Cancelling is free up to 3 hours before your session.
